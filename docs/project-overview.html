Unified Healthcare Management Solution

A single-facility hospital platform that puts patient records, appointments and the live queue, digital prescriptions, diagnostic lab orders and reports, billing and hospital communication under one roof — with role-based dashboards for administrators, doctors, nurses, diagnostic staff and patients. Every route and action is gated by role, clinical reads are gated by relationship, and a patient's portal always resolves the patient from the session, never from a URL.

  • Next.js 16 (App Router)
  • React 19
  • TypeScript (strict)
  • Tailwind CSS v4
  • MongoDB + Mongoose
  • jose session JWT

English-only · scoped to a single facility · steps 0–21 of the plan complete.

Section 2 The five roles

One shell, five dashboards. What each role can see follows from what it is entitled to, not from what happens to be convenient.

Administrator

Runs the hospital's record of itself — staff accounts, departments, the price list, invoices, analytics and the audit trail. Manages the institution, not its patients' records.

Doctor

Consults, diagnoses, prescribes and orders tests. Reads a patient's timeline only through an existing clinical relationship — access by relationship, not by role alone.

Nurse

Checks patients in, records vitals at the door and triages — an urgent case moves ahead of token order in the queue.

Diagnostic lab

Takes the orders addressed to it, uploads the report file and releases the result. Sees its own orders and the reports it produced — nothing else.

Patient

A personal portal: appointments, records, prescriptions, lab results and billing — always their own, resolved from the session and never from a URL.

Section 3 The request lifecycle

Every request crosses the same checkpoints in the same order, and each hop discharges one security obligation. Nothing downstream has to re-ask a question a hop upstream already answered.

Section 4 How a write travels the layers

Each layer does one job and refuses to do its neighbour's. The action gates and validates but holds no business logic; the service owns the rules; the model is the last line that can say no.

Section 5 The data model

Eighteen Mongoose models, drawn along one deliberate line: User answers "who is asking" and is read on every sign-in; Patient answers "what is known about them" and is read deliberately. Keeping allergies, conditions and blood group off the account keeps clinical data out of the document the session token is minted from — and is what lets the audit trail treat a clinical read as distinct from an ordinary one.

Section 6 The clinical workflow, end to end

One patient's journey through the system, from first registration to a settled bill. Every stage is a state the records actually move through — nothing here is decorative.

Access by relationship A doctor reads only the patients they treat; wrong-owner access to a clinical record returns 404, not 403 — confirming a record exists is itself a disclosure.
Every clinical read audited An append-only AuditLog records who read which clinical record and when — the trail an administrator can see, distinct from the contents they cannot.
Rate limits on public endpoints Database-backed throttles on sign-in, registration and password reset, with sign-in failure counting behind them.
